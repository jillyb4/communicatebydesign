How to use this

Three tabs at the top of the page:

  • Print symbol cards — type words, get a page of picture cards, cut them out.
  • First–Then board — two boxes: what is happening now, and what comes next.
  • Routine & timers — the same idea stretched to as many steps as the routine actually has, with minutes on the steps that need them.

Everything works printed, on a device, or both. Nothing needs installing.

Making a sheet

  1. Type your words in the box below — one per line, or separated by commas.
  2. Click Add words. A picture is found for each one.
  3. If a picture is wrong, click Other symbols on that card and pick a better one, or type a different word in the small search box. The printed label stays whatever you typed.
  4. Set the card size and how much space you want for cutting.
  5. Click Print sheet.

One thing that matters when you print

In the print window, set Scale to 100% — not "Fit to page." Fit to page shrinks everything, and the cards stop being the size they say they are.

Good to know

  • You need internet the first time you look up a word — that is how the pictures are found.
  • Nothing is saved anywhere but your own browser. No account, no upload, no student information leaves this page. The only thing sent out is the vocabulary word itself, to look up a picture for it.
  • Card borders are colored by word type — green for actions, gray for things, yellow for people, orange for describing words, blue for little words, pink for social words. Cut apart, the color helps show how words fit together.
  • Check the student's own system first. If they already use a device, book, or cards, match those pictures where you can. A student who learned one picture for "break" should not have to learn a second one.
  • A student may select a card, point to it, use gaze to indicate it, hand it to you, or answer some other way entirely. All of it counts.

The First–Then board (second tab)

Two boxes: what is happening now, and what comes next. Print it and laminate it, or open it on a device where tapping a box says it out loud.

  • Labels are yours to set. First / Then, Now / Next, or your own words. Use whatever this student already knows — a student who learned one wording should not have to learn a second.
  • Two different jobs. With the adult setting both boxes, the board tells a student what is coming — it helps with transitions, and it is not the student communicating. Turn on let the student choose what comes next and the second box becomes something else entirely: the student is now telling you something.
  • If they choose, honor it. A choice that gets overridden teaches that choosing does not matter, which is the opposite of the point.
  • Two to six options. More than six stops being a choice and starts being a search.
  • Two pictures in one box. Type calm, body and both pictures go in the same box, read as one message — the way a sentence strip works. Up to three.
  • On an iPad: add the page to the Home Screen first, then turn on Guided Access. Opening a downloaded file directly in Safari works but is fiddly.

Routine & timers (third tab)

A First–Then board is two boxes. A lot of what a day asks of a student is a chain of them — calm the body, take a break, say where you are going, pick what happens when you get there. That is this tab.

  • Each step gets a name and pictures. A comma puts two pictures in one box. Up to ten steps.
  • Any step can be the student's. Tick the student chooses this step and that step becomes a choice board — they fill it, and what they pick goes in the choice log below.
  • Minutes are optional. Leave a step at 0 if it runs until it is finished rather than until a clock says so.
  • The timer does not end the step — you do. When it reaches zero it says "time's up" and stops there. A student who is not regulated yet needs the break to keep going, and a clock cannot see that. There is a +1 min button for exactly that reason, and no timer value is ever written to the log. How long a student needed is not data about the student.
  • On a device: the whole strip shows across the top so the student can see what is still coming, with the current step large underneath. Tapping the big box says it out loud. You move it on with Next step, or tap any step in the strip to jump there.
  • Printed: the strip prints top to bottom with the minutes marked, and each choice step's options print underneath as loose cards to cut out and velcro on.

The choice log

When a student picks something — on either board — it is recorded: the word, the time, what it was chosen from, and how long it took. One log, because it is one student's language. It is a record of what they communicated, not a score.

  • Nothing is marked right or wrong, because there is no correct answer to a choice. You will not find an accuracy figure anywhere in this tool.
  • Mark it spontaneous or prompted from the buttons under the board. Language becoming self-initiated is the thing worth watching over months.
  • "Could not" is about the room, not the student. If choices often cannot be honored, the options on the board are the thing to change.
  • "Took" is not a target. Time usually shortens as a board becomes familiar, but a long pause is often a student weighing two things they want, and a fast tap can be a reach for whatever was nearest. Watch it across weeks in the same setup, never coach for speed, and when it shifts look at what changed around the student first.
  • It stays on this device. Nothing is uploaded. The CSV is written only when you click Export. Use initials or a code, not a full name. The CSV has no room for the note above — if you hand the file to someone, hand them the note too.

If the pictures do not appear

You are probably offline, or a school network is blocking the picture site. Cards will still print as empty boxes you can draw in. Reconnect and click Retry lookups.
